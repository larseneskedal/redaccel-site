How can I track what AI search engines say about my brand?

Updated November 2026 4 min read By Redaccel
Extract redaccel.com · answer · November 2026

Track what AI engines say about your brand by running a fixed set of your buyers' questions on each engine every day, and recording three things per answer: whether you were named, in what position and tone, and which pages the engine cited. A single run tells you almost nothing because AI answers change from one run to the next; a share over hundreds of runs is the number that means something. You can do this by hand for a week, read Google's own AI report in Search Console for free, or pay a tracker to sample daily.

Why one screenshot is not tracking

In a study of 2,961 runs across 12 prompts, the same AI engines named different brands for the same question within the same hour[1]. So the question "does ChatGPT recommend us?" has no yes-or-no answer. The honest version is "in what share of runs on this question does ChatGPT name us, and who does it name instead?" That is a sampling problem, and everything below is a way of sampling.

What to measure in each answer

  • Mention. Were you named in the prose of the answer? Count a brand named only inside a cited link title separately; it is not a recommendation.
  • Position and tone. First name listed or an afterthought; recommended, mentioned, or warned against.
  • Competitors. Which other brands were named. Share of voice is your mentions divided by all tracked-brand mentions, and it is the number that moves when work lands.
  • Citations. Every URL the engine retrieved or linked. This is the most useful column, because the pages that keep showing up are the pages that decide the answer, and the ones you need to get onto.

The prompt set you choose decides what the numbers mean, so choose it before you compare tools.

Build the prompt set first

The prompt set is the measurement. Write the questions your buyers actually type: "best X for Y", "X vs Z", "is X worth it", "X alternatives". Keep branded questions ("is Acme legit") in a separate group, because your name is in the question and the numbers mean something different. Then freeze the set. A set that changes month to month produces movement that is the change in questions, not in your visibility. How a prompt is phrased changes who gets named: a study of 37,804 responses found brand mentions shifting with prompt style alone[4], which is one more reason to fix the wording and leave it.

Know what your tool is actually reading

Trackers reach the engines in two ways: through the consumer product, which searches the live web, or through an API, which often does not. The two give different answers. In one comparison of 13,779 answers, the API and the scraped product disagreed on citations and brand mentions often enough to change a report[5]. For a visibility read, you want the version your buyers use, which is the product with web search on. Ask the vendor which one you are getting.

How often, and for how long

Daily is the useful cadence: engines re-read sources on their own schedules, and a daily sample catches the day a new placement starts being cited. Report monthly, as a like-for-like comparison on the frozen set. Expect movement on one or two engines before the others, because each reads a different set of pages: Google's AI Overviews take about 38 percent of their citations from the top 10 results[6], while only about 12 percent of the URLs ChatGPT cites rank there[7].

Read the citations, not just the score

A visibility score tells you where you stand; the citation log tells you what to do about it. Sort cited URLs by how often they appear across your prompt set over 30 days. In our category the top of that list was third-party lists and review directories, and one "best agencies" article alone was cited 562 times in a month[8]. Those pages are the target list. Owned pages, by contrast, were 9 of 200 citations in our ChatGPT audit[8]: worth fixing, not worth building a plan around.

Frequently asked

How can I track what AI search engines say about my brand?

Run a fixed set of your buyers' questions on each engine daily and record, per answer, whether you were named, in what position and tone, which competitors were named, and which pages were cited. Do it by hand for a week, read Search Console's AI report for Google, or use a tracker such as Peec AI, Profound, Otterly, Semrush or Ahrefs Brand Radar for daily sampling.

Can I see AI traffic in Google Search Console?

Yes. Since June 2026, worldwide since 31 August 2026, the Performance report can show impressions and clicks from AI Overviews and AI Mode for your own site. It does not show whether you were named in the answer or who else was, and it covers Google only.

How many prompts do I need to track?

Twenty to forty unbranded buyer questions is enough for most brands, plus a handful of branded ones kept separate. Freeze the set once it is written; a changing set produces movement that is not visibility.

Why do my results differ from my tracker's?

Usually because one of you is reading the API and the other the consumer product with web search, and they cite different pages. Or because a single run is being compared with a share over many runs. Ask what the tool reads and compare shares, not screenshots.
