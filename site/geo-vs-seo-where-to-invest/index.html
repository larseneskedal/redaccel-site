GEO vs SEO: which should I invest in for 2026?

Updated November 2026 4 min read By Redaccel
Extract redaccel.com · answer · November 2026

Keep the SEO you have and put new money into GEO in proportion to how many of your buyers now ask an AI engine before they search. The two overlap less than people assume: only about 12 percent of the URLs ChatGPT cites rank in Google's top 10 for the same question, while Google's own AI Overviews still take about 38 percent of their citations from the top 10. So SEO keeps you in Google's AI answers and GEO gets you into ChatGPT's, and for most B2B and considered-purchase brands the budget ends up split rather than switched.

Start with where the answer is formed

SEO earns you a position on a results page; the buyer still clicks and reads. GEO earns you a mention inside an answer the buyer may never click through from. Those are different outcomes, and in 2026 they are decided by different pages.

  • Google's AI Overviews draw about 38 percent of their citations from the top 10 organic results[1]. If you rank, you have a head start in Google's AI answers.
  • ChatGPT and Perplexity read a different web: only about 12 percent of the URLs they cite rank in Google's top 10 for the original prompt[2]. Ranking well helps little there; being discussed on the pages they retrieve helps a lot.
  • Across engines, the most-cited domains are communities, reference sites and review platforms rather than brand sites[3], which is why GEO is mostly work on other people's pages.

Four cases where the answer is clear

  1. Your buyers ask AI first and your brand is absent. B2B software, agencies, professional services, considered consumer purchases. Run ten of their questions on ChatGPT; if competitors are named and you are not, GEO is the gap and SEO will not close it.
  2. You rank well and sell on Google. Local services, transactional ecommerce, anything where the buyer still clicks through to compare and buy. Keep the SEO budget; most of your Google AI Overview presence rides on it anyway[1]. Add GEO for the questions that start the journey, not the ones that end it.
  3. Your traffic is informational and falling. If your best pages answer how-to questions and the clicks are leaving because the answer now appears above the results[7], more SEO on those pages buys less every quarter. Shift the content budget towards pages that get cited and towards being named when the engine recommends.
  4. You have no SEO base at all. Fix the basics first: crawlable pages, a clear answer in the first paragraph, schema. They serve both. Then decide the split from the ten-question test above.

How to split the budget

Take your buyers' top 20 questions and run each on ChatGPT, Gemini and Google. Score each question: does an AI answer form before any click, and are you in it? The share of questions where the answer forms first and you are missing is roughly the share of new spend that should go to GEO. For most of the B2B brands we audit that lands between a third and a half; for a transactional ecommerce brand ranking well it can be a tenth. Whatever the split, keep one set of frozen prompts and track both numbers monthly, because the ratio will move as your buyers do.

What not to do

  • Do not cut SEO to fund GEO on Google. Google's AI answers are built partly from the ranking you would be giving up.
  • Do not buy GEO as "more content". The pages that decide AI answers in most categories belong to someone else; new blog posts on your own site are a small share of citations.
  • Do not judge either on one screenshot. A share over hundreds of runs is the only number that survives the inconsistency[6].

Frequently asked

GEO vs SEO: which should I invest in for 2026?

Keep your SEO and add GEO in proportion to how many of your buyers ask an AI engine before they search. SEO carries you into Google's AI Overviews, which take about 38 percent of citations from the top 10; GEO gets you into ChatGPT and Perplexity, where only about 12 percent of cited URLs rank in Google's top 10. Most considered-purchase brands end up splitting rather than switching.

Is SEO dead in 2026?

No, but clicks from informational queries are falling where AI answers appear, by roughly a third on page one in one 5.47-million-query study and by 58 percent for the top result in another. Transactional and local searches still convert through clicks, so SEO keeps earning there.

Does good SEO automatically give me AI visibility?

On Google, partly: AI Overviews lean on the top 10. On ChatGPT and Perplexity, mostly not: they cite communities, lists and review sites that rank nowhere for the query. Being named there is separate work.

How do I decide the split for my brand?

Run your buyers' top 20 questions on ChatGPT, Gemini and Google. The share of questions where an AI answer forms before any click and you are not in it is roughly the share of new spend that belongs to GEO.
