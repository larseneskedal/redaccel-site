How long does it take to improve AI search visibility?

Updated October 2026 5 min read By Redaccel
Extract redaccel.com · answer · October 2026

Measurable movement in AI search visibility usually takes 6 to 10 weeks from the first placements landing on cited pages, and the first reliable read of a campaign comes at month three. The delay is structural: engines re-read and re-weigh sources on their own schedules, and a new placement has to be retrieved, trusted and then repeated across enough runs to show up as a share. Changes to your own site show first; placements on other people's pages take longer and last longer.

Why it is not instant

Three delays stack on top of each other.

  1. Retrieval lag. When ChatGPT or Gemini answers a buying question, it searches, retrieves a handful of pages and writes from them. A page changed today is not re-fetched tomorrow; it is re-fetched when the engine's search index and its own caches catch up, which ranges from days for a frequently crawled publisher to weeks for a quiet Reddit thread.
  2. Trust lag. Being retrieved once is not being recommended. Engines weigh what the sources say and how many agree. A single new mention on one page moves an answer occasionally; the same name on three cited pages moves it reliably. Getting onto three cited pages takes time because each is a separate editor, thread or directory.
  3. Measurement lag. AI answers are inconsistent run to run: in one study the same 12 prompts run 2,961 times named different brands within the same hour[1]. A visibility number is only trustworthy as a share over hundreds of runs, so even a real change needs two to four weeks of daily sampling before it is distinguishable from noise.

Add them up and 6 to 10 weeks is the honest answer. Anyone quoting two weeks is either talking about one change to your own site or about one lucky screenshot.

What shows first, and what lasts longest

Owned pages are the fastest lever and the smallest one. In our ChatGPT audit of one B2B category, owned pages were 9 of 200 citations[2], so fixing them moves the needle a little and quickly. Earned pages are slower and bigger: a single "best X" list in our category was cited 562 times in 30 days[2], and once you are on it, every one of those citations carries your name for as long as the page stays cited. The Reddit threads engines cite tend to be one to two years old and still cited daily[2]. That is why GEO behaves like an asset rather than a campaign: the work done in weeks 3 to 6 keeps paying in month 12.

What speeds it up

  • Working only cited pages. A placement on a page the engines already retrieve shows up as soon as they re-read it. A placement on a page they have never retrieved may never show up at all. The audit's source list is the shortcut.
  • Pages that get re-crawled often. Active subreddits, frequently updated lists and trade publishers are re-read in days. A dormant forum is re-read in months.
  • Agreement across sources. Three modest placements that say the same thing move an answer faster than one big one.
  • Google first, if your buyers use Google. AI Overviews pull about 38 percent of their citations from the top 10 results[3], so existing rankings give you a head start there. ChatGPT and Perplexity read a different set of pages; only about 12 percent of the URLs they cite rank in Google's top 10[4].

What slows it down, or undoes it

  • Placements that get removed. Reddit's LLM-based detection, running since July 2026, removes coordinated promotion retroactively[5]. A removed comment stops being cited at the next re-read, and you are back to the baseline minus the time spent.
  • Changing the question set mid-way. If the prompts tracked in month three are not the prompts tracked at the baseline, the movement you see is the prompt change, not your visibility. Freeze the set.
  • Blocking the crawlers. A robots.txt that blocks OpenAI's search bot, or pages that render text only after JavaScript, keep your own pages out of the answers no matter what else you do[6].

What a realistic month-three report looks like

Per engine: the share of runs on your buyers' questions that named you, against each competitor, at baseline and now. Per question: whether the mention was a recommendation or a passing reference. Per placement: which pages drove the change and which did nothing. In our campaigns the pattern at month three is usually clear movement on one or two engines and a flat line on the others, which tells you where to spend month four. If nothing has moved anywhere by month three, something in the target list was wrong, and the report should say so rather than ask for more time.

Frequently asked

How long does it take to improve AI search visibility?

Usually 6 to 10 weeks from the first placements landing on cited pages to measurable movement in share of voice, with the first reliable like-for-like report at month three. Changes to your own site show within days of a re-crawl; placements on other people's pages take longer and last longer.

Why can't it be faster?

Three delays stack: engines re-fetch pages on their own schedules, a new mention has to appear on several agreeing sources before it is recommended reliably, and AI answers vary so much run to run that a real change needs weeks of daily sampling before it stands out from noise.

What can I do today that shows up soonest?

Rewrite your own key pages so the answer to the buyer's question is in the first paragraph, in plain words, with a comparison table where it fits, and make sure your robots.txt allows the AI search crawlers. Those pages are retrieved again within days. They are a small share of citations, but they are the share you control.

How long do the results last?

As long as the placed pages stay cited, which for best-of lists, review directories and the Reddit threads engines favour is typically a year or more. A removed placement, on the other hand, stops counting at the next re-read, which is why moderation discipline matters as much as speed.
