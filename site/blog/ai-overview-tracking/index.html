AI Overview tracking: how to see if you appear, how wrong each method can be, and what a citation does to clicks

Updated October 2026 7 min read By Redaccel
Extract redaccel.com · measurement · October 2026

To track AI Overviews you need two sources: Google Search Console's generative AI report, which since 31 August 2026 shows every site its AI impressions by page but no clicks or queries, and a third-party tracker that checks your keywords repeatedly.[1] It is worth tracking because citations move clicks: in Seer Interactive's 2025 data, brands cited in an AI Overview averaged 2.07% organic CTR on informational queries, against 0.94% for brands left out[5].

What is the difference between AI Overviews and AI Mode?

AI Overviews are the AI summary that sits on top of a normal results page; AI Mode is a separate, conversational search where the whole answer is AI-generated. Google says AI Overviews are shown only when its systems judge them additive to classic Search, "and as such, often don't trigger", while AI Mode is aimed at queries that need further exploration, reasoning or comparison[4].

Both may use "query fan-out", issuing several related searches across subtopics to build one answer, and Google says the two "may use different models and techniques, so the set of responses and links they show will vary"[4]. Being cited in one tells you little about the other.

They are also counted differently. In an AI Overview, every link shares a single position on the results page. In AI Mode, standard position rules apply, and a follow-up question counts as a brand new query with its own impressions and clicks[3]. A page can look "position 1" in an AI Overview while sitting fifth in the list of sources.

Can Google Search Console show if you appear in AI Overviews?

Yes, at page level, but without clicks or queries. Google launched Search Generative AI performance reports on 3 June 2026 and, as of 31 August 2026, has rolled them out to all websites worldwide[1]. The Search version shows impressions, pages, countries, devices and dates, with hourly to monthly granularity[1].

The limits matter more than the features:

  • No clicks or CTR. The launch post lists impressions only, and says Google may add metrics over time[1].
  • No query dimension. The help page lists pages, countries, dates and devices. It does not list queries, so you can see which URL was shown but not for what[2].
  • AI Overviews and AI Mode are combined. The report covers both features, and the help page describes no filter to separate them[2].
  • Deduplicated totals. If two of your pages appear in the same AI feature, the chart counts one impression[2].
  • A volume floor. Sites with too few AI impressions do not see the report at all[2].

Clicks do exist, just not there. A click on a link inside an AI Overview or AI Mode counts as a click in the normal Performance report, under the "Web" search type[3], where it is blended with every blue-link click. So Google gives you AI impressions on one screen and AI clicks mixed into another. A clean AI Overview CTR for your own site is not something Google reports today.

Which tracking methods exist, and how wrong can each one be?

Every method answers a narrower question than it appears to, so combine at least two. This is how we compare them.

The variance is the part people underrate. SparkToro and Gumshoe had 600 volunteers run 12 prompts through ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI Overviews (or AI Mode when no Overview showed) a combined 2,961 times. For ChatGPT and Google's AI, the chance of getting the same list of brands in any two responses was under 1 in 100[8]. One screenshot of an AI Overview proves that you appeared once. It does not prove you appear.

Even large studies carry this error. Seer Interactive, whose CTR data we use below, lists among its own limits that it "cannot track AIO in real time" and that its static label of whether a query shows an AI Overview is "a known limitation"[5]. Does being cited in an AI Overview increase clicks?

Yes compared with being left out, no compared with a results page that has no AI Overview at all. Seer Interactive's 2026 update covers 53 brands, 5.47 million tracked queries and 2.43 billion organic impressions, with full-year 2025 data plus early 2026[5].

Informational queries, full-year 2025 average CTR by AI Overview citation status (Seer Interactive, published April 2026).
Status on the results pageOrganic CTRPaid CTR
No AI Overview3.35%19.75%
AI Overview, your brand cited2.07%15.74%
AI Overview, your brand not cited0.94%11.19%

Two caveats from Seer itself[5]. First, one account made up 47% of the "not cited" impressions at a 0.20% CTR; without it, that row is roughly 1.61%. The gap between cited and uncited is real in this data but smaller than the headline. Second, Seer says it cannot claim causation, because higher-authority brands are also more likely to be cited. Some of the "citation effect" may simply be a strong-brand effect.

Seer also reports that organic CTR on queries with an AI Overview rose from a low of 1.3% in December 2025 to 2.4% in February 2026, after more than a year of decline[5]. Two months is a short window and Seer's own forecast model had predicted further decline, so treat this as a direction to watch, not a recovery.

How much do AI Overviews reduce clicks overall?

A lot, though the size depends on who measured, when and how. Ahrefs compared 300,000 keywords using aggregated Search Console desktop data. Average position 1 CTR on keywords that showed an AI Overview fell from 0.073 in December 2023 to 0.016 in December 2025, which Ahrefs estimates is about 58% lower than it would have been without the AI Overview. Its April 2025 run of the same method found 34.5%[6].

Pew Research Center measured behaviour rather than CTR. Tracking the browsing of 900 US adults in March 2025, it found users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits to a results page with an AI summary, against 15% without one. A link inside the summary was clicked on just 1% of those visits[7]. That data is now 18 months old, so read it as the early baseline.

Google's position is different. It says clicks from results pages with AI Overviews are "higher quality", meaning people spend more time on the site[4]. Google has not published the data behind that claim, so we class it as unverified, not false.

We have not found a public, large-sample study of click-through rates from AI Mode as of October 2026. Anyone quoting an AI Mode CTR should be able to name the sample.

How do you appear in AI Overviews?

By Google's account, with ordinary SEO: there are no extra requirements. A page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, and Google says you do not need special schema, AI text files or new markup[4]. The reverse also holds: nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex limit what can be shown[4], so check that a template has not quietly excluded you.

Beyond that, separate what is evidenced from what is inferred. Evidenced: the fan-out searches subtopics, and Google says this surfaces a wider and more diverse set of links than a classic web search[4]. Inferred, from our own work, not a published study: pages that answer a specific sub-question in their first sentence, and cover the comparisons a buyer would ask next, are easier to cite. We apply that method, but we have no controlled test that isolates its effect, and you should be wary of anyone who claims one. Our piece on how GEO differs from SEO covers where the two overlap.

What should an AI Overview tracking setup look like?

Start with Google's data, add sampling, and report rates with dates. This is the setup we use:

  1. Export the generative AI report weekly, by page. It is the only first-party record of your AI impressions, and exports turn unavailable values into zeros[2], so note gaps.
  2. Join it to the Performance report for the same pages. If AI impressions rise while clicks fall, you are probably being shown without being chosen.
  3. Flag AI Overview presence on your keyword list in a rank tracker, and record the share of keywords where you are cited, not a yes or no.
  4. Sample your priority prompts repeatedly, including in AI Mode, which Search Console does not split out. Our guide to measuring AI visibility explains prompt sets and run counts.
  5. Put the month on every number you report. The 34.5% and 58% figures above came from the same method a year apart.

Frequently asked

Does Google Search Console show AI Overview data?

Yes. Since 31 August 2026 every site has access to a generative AI performance report showing impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode by page, country, device and date. It does not show clicks, CTR or queries, and it combines the two features. Clicks from AI features are counted in the main Performance report, mixed in with ordinary blue-link clicks.

Is AI Mode tracked separately from AI Overviews?

Not in Search Console's generative AI report, which covers both together. The two also count differently: every link in an AI Overview shares one position, while AI Mode uses standard position rules and treats each follow-up question as a new query. To see AI Mode on its own, you need a third-party tracker that runs your prompts there repeatedly.

What CTR can I expect if I am cited in an AI Overview?

In Seer Interactive's 2025 data for informational queries, cited brands averaged 2.07% organic CTR, uncited brands 0.94% and queries with no AI Overview 3.35%. Without one large account the uncited figure is about 1.61%. Seer says this is correlation, since stronger brands are cited more, so treat it as a range rather than a forecast for your site.

How do I get my site into AI Overviews?

Google says there are no extra requirements beyond normal SEO: the page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet, and no special schema or AI text file is needed. Check that nosnippet or noindex is not excluding key pages. Beyond that, clear answers to specific sub-questions help in our experience, though no public controlled study has proved it.
