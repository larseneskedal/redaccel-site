How to rank in ChatGPT: how retrieval and selection work, and the levers that exist

Updated November 2026 7 min read By Redaccel
Extract redaccel.com · strategy · November 2026

You cannot rank in ChatGPT in the way you rank in Google, because there is no stable list to climb: ChatGPT rewrites the question, retrieves a pool of pages that changes from run to run, and selects a handful to cite. In a 2026 study of 1,600 runs per model, GPT-5.5 Instant retrieved about 23 candidate pages per answer and cited about 6.5, and 44.5% of candidate URLs appeared in only 1 of 20 identical runs[1]. The job is to be retrieved often and chosen when you are.

Can you rank in ChatGPT at all?

No, not in the sense of holding position 3 for a keyword. ChatGPT produces a new answer every time, built from whatever its search step returned on that run, so there is no page of ten blue links that you can sit on. What you can do is raise the probability that your pages are retrieved and then picked as a source. That probability is measurable, and several things move it.

The clearest public evidence comes from a preprint by researchers at the National University of Singapore and Xi'an Jiaotong University, published on 11 October 2026. They asked 80 questions 20 times each to ChatGPT 5.3 Instant and 5.5 Instant between 29 March and 29 July 2026, and logged both the pages ChatGPT retrieved and the pages it cited[1]. It is not peer reviewed, and 80 questions is a modest sample, but it is the only study we know of that separates retrieval from citation inside production ChatGPT. Most of what follows leans on it, with the labels left on.

How does ChatGPT search actually retrieve sources?

It retrieves in three steps: decide whether to search, write its own queries, then pull candidates and choose a few to cite. Each step is a filter, and each one can drop you.

Step 1: decide whether to search. Often it does not. Ahrefs analysed 66.6 million free, logged-out GPT-5.6 Luna responses from August and September 2026 and found that only 14.4% contained fan-out queries, at about 1.6 queries each[2]. Ahrefs notes that paid and reasoning models search more. Separately, Goodie, which sells AI search analytics, tracked 94,851 ChatGPT answers and saw the share with no sources at all jump from about 1.5% to about 12.2% on 31 October 2026[3]. Goodie's leading explanation is that ChatGPT searched less, which OpenAI has not confirmed. When there is no search, the answer comes from what the model learned in training, and no page gets cited.

Step 2: rewrite the question. ChatGPT does not search for your customer's words. In the 2026 study, none of the GPT-5.3 Instant or GPT-5.5 Instant runs issued a search query identical to the user's question; for the older GPT-5.2 Instant it was 5.5% of runs[1]. Ahrefs found the rewritten queries drifting toward words like "official" (10.1% to 13.1% of English fan-outs from August to September 2026) and "price" (6.0% to 8.9%), and 6.8% of fan-outs used a site: operator to search one domain directly[2].

Step 3: retrieve, then select. GPT-5.5 Instant pulled an average of 23.47 candidate pages per run and cited 6.53. GPT-5.3 Instant pulled 18.74 and cited 5.55[1]. So roughly 1 in 3 or 4 retrieved pages makes it into the answer. Being retrieved is necessary. It is not enough.

Why is "rank" the wrong model for ChatGPT?

Because the pool of candidates changes almost every time the same question is asked. Across 20 identical runs, 40.8% (GPT-5.3) and 44.5% (GPT-5.5) of retrieved URLs appeared only once, and only 9.4% and 7.7% made it into a stable core seen in at least 14 runs[1]. One question produced 96 to 132 distinct URLs on average over those 20 runs.

The final answers vary just as much. SparkToro and Gumshoe had 600 volunteers run 12 prompts 2,961 times across ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI in November and December 2025, and found less than a 1 in 100 chance that ChatGPT or Google's AI would return the same list of brands twice, and about 1 in 1,000 for the same list in the same order[4].

Google rankings are a weak stand-in, too. Google's results contained only 9.4% (GPT-5.3) and 6.4% (GPT-5.5) of the exact URLs ChatGPT cited, and even Google plus OpenAI's own Responses API recovered just 28.3% and 26.2%[1]. In mid-2025, Ahrefs found that 8.0% of ChatGPT's in-text citations ranked in Google's top 10 for the same prompt, across 15,000 long-tail queries[5]. Good SEO still helps you get into the pool. It does not tell you whether you will be picked.

The useful replacement for "what position am I?" is "in what share of runs am I retrieved, and in what share am I cited?". That is a rate, not a rank, and it only means something across many runs. Our guide to measuring AI visibility covers how to sample it.

What are the levers that actually exist?

There are about six, and they split into two groups: getting retrieved and getting selected. The table shows each one with how strong the evidence is today.

How do you get retrieved more often?

Start with access, then cover the queries the model writes rather than the ones your customer types. OpenAI's documentation says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, apart from navigational links, and that robots.txt changes take about 24 hours to apply[6]. GPTBot is a different crawler used for training, so blocking it does not remove you from search. Check that your CDN or firewall is not blocking OAI-SearchBot either; a robots.txt allow is useless if the request never reaches you.

Next, think in sub-queries. If a buyer asks "what is the best invoicing tool for a small agency", ChatGPT may search for prices, reviews, comparisons and official product pages. A pricing page that states the price in text, a comparison page that names alternatives fairly and a product page that says plainly what you do each give it something to retrieve. This is the core of generative engine optimisation, and our LLM SEO playbook goes deeper on structure.

Finally, aim for depth across a topic. The 2026 study found that items retrieved more often across runs were more likely to be cited, and that the signal was stronger at the hostname and entity level than for single URLs[1]. Our reading, which is inference, is that a site that covers a subject from several angles gets more chances to land in a shifting pool than a site with one strong page.

How do you get selected once you are retrieved?

Give the model a passage it can lift without editing. In the 2026 study, cited snippets were far more often long, self-contained blocks than uncited ones, and snippet length on its own predicted citation with an AUC of 0.61 to 0.63[1]. That is a modest signal and a correlation, so treat it as a writing habit rather than a rule: answer the question in the first sentence of a section, keep the claim and its evidence together, and name the year on every figure.

Read more: position inside the candidate list matters less than you would think

Candidates that sat beyond position 15 in ChatGPT's retrieved list were still cited 24.0% (GPT-5.3) and 19.0% (GPT-5.5) of the time[1]. Being near the top of the pool helps, but the selection step clearly weighs content, not just order. This is one more reason the word "rank" misleads.

Selection also happens at the level of who is mentioned, not only which URL is linked. Both proxies in the study matched ChatGPT's choices better at the entity level than at the exact-URL level[1]. In plain terms, being named on the pages ChatGPT trusts in your category can count as much as your own page. Which pages those are changes quickly: Ahrefs' Brand Radar data showed PubMed rising from 64th to 4th among ChatGPT's most-cited domains between March and September 2026, while Reddit citations fell 88% over the same period[2]. Check your own category before you copy someone else's source list.

What does not work, or is unproven?

Anything sold as a ChatGPT ranking factor list with precise weights. OpenAI does not publish its retrieval or selection logic, and the public studies are observational. Three common claims to treat with care:

  • "Rank top 3 in Google and you are in." The overlap figures above say otherwise for ChatGPT.
  • "One check proves it worked." With most retrieved URLs appearing in a single run, one screenshot is noise[1].
  • "This tactic is permanent." ChatGPT's behaviour changed visibly within a single day in October 2026[3]. Ahrefs calls its own findings a snapshot of one model[2].

Nobody has yet published a controlled test showing that a given content change raises ChatGPT citation rates by a given amount. That is the honest state of the field in November 2026.

How should you measure progress?

Track retrieval and citation rates across a fixed set of prompts, run many times, over weeks. Pick 20 to 50 questions your buyers actually ask, run each repeatedly, and record whether you were cited, whether you were mentioned without a link, and which domains were cited instead. Compare month to month, and expect noise. If you want a starting point, our free AI visibility audit runs that baseline for your category.

Frequently asked

How do I rank in ChatGPT?

You do not rank in ChatGPT in the Google sense. It rewrites the question, retrieves a pool of pages that changes from run to run and cites a few. To be cited more often, allow OAI-SearchBot, cover the sub-queries the model writes (prices, reviews, comparisons, official pages), build depth across your topic and write self-contained passages it can quote.

Does ranking on Google help me get cited by ChatGPT?

It helps you get into the candidate pool, but it is a weak predictor of citation. In a 2026 study, Google's results contained only 6.4% to 9.4% of the exact URLs ChatGPT cited. Ahrefs found in 2025 that 8.0% of ChatGPT's in-text citations ranked in Google's top 10 for the same prompt. Treat SEO as the foundation, not the finish line.

Does ChatGPT search the web for every question?

No. Ahrefs found that only 14.4% of 66.6 million free, logged-out ChatGPT responses from August and September 2026 contained fan-out search queries. Paid and reasoning models search more. When ChatGPT does not search, it answers from training data and cites no pages, so your broader presence on the open web matters too.

What are the ChatGPT ranking factors?

OpenAI does not publish any. Public research points to signals that correlate with citation: being retrieved repeatedly across runs, being accessible to OAI-SearchBot, offering long self-contained passages and being named on sources ChatGPT trusts in your category. These are observational findings from 2025 and 2026, not confirmed factors, and they shift as OpenAI updates its models.

How long does it take to start getting ChatGPT citations?

Nobody has published reliable timelines. Crawler access changes apply in about 24 hours according to OpenAI, but whether new content gets retrieved and selected depends on the queries ChatGPT writes and the competition in the pool. Measure across repeated runs for several weeks before judging any change, because single checks are dominated by run-to-run variation.
