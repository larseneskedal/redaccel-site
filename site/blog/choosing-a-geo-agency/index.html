How to choose a GEO agency: the questions that expose a fake one

Updated October 2026 7 min read By Redaccel
Extract redaccel.com · buyer's guide · October 2026

To choose a GEO agency, ask for three things before you sign: a baseline of your share of voice across named AI engines, the exact prompt list behind it, and a plan to re-run those same prompts within a quarter. One screenshot proves nothing, because in SparkToro's 2025 test the same prompt returned the same list of brands less than 1 time in 100[1]. We sell GEO, so read this with that bias in mind.

Should you trust a buyer's guide written by a GEO agency?

Only as far as it gives you checks you can run without us. Redaccel sells GEO retainers, so we have an interest in you hiring someone. What follows is the checklist we would want a buyer to use on us, including the parts that argue for hiring nobody. Every figure is linked and dated, and where nobody has measured something, we say so.

How we measure, so you can hold us to the same standard: we run a fixed set of buyer questions every day across ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews and report 30-day totals, because single answers vary from run to run. In our set for this category (28 unbranded buyer questions, 25 August to 24 September 2026, 2,594 answers), the engines mostly named four agencies, drawn from a small set of list pages[5]. You can read the full method on our GEO agencies compared page.

What is the difference between a GEO agency and an SEO agency?

An SEO agency works to move your page up a ranked list; a GEO agency works to get your brand named and cited inside an AI answer, which is a paragraph with no positions. If you need the background, start with what GEO is and how it relates to GEO versus SEO.

The overlap is real. Google's own guide says SEO best practices "continue to be relevant" because its generative AI features "are rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems"[3]. But ranking well no longer means being cited. In Ahrefs' March 2026 analysis of 863,000 keyword results and 4 million AI Overview URLs, 37.9% of cited URLs also ranked in the top 10, down from about 76% in its July 2025 study[2]. Roughly 31% of citations came from pages ranked 11 to 100, and another 31% from pages beyond the top 100[2].

That gap is the case for GEO. It is not, by itself, a case for a new agency.

When should you not hire a GEO agency at all?

Skip the agency if your existing SEO program is healthy and nobody has measured a gap in AI answers yet. Measure first. In our experience, these are the situations where hiring anyone is premature:

  • You have no baseline. If you don't know how often engines name you today, you cannot tell whether an agency helped. A one-off audit costs less than three months of retainer.
  • Your buyers don't ask AI. Some categories, such as local trades or very small B2B niches, may see few AI-assisted searches. Check your analytics for AI referrals before you assume otherwise.
  • Your site has basic problems. If pages are not indexed or load badly, fix that first. Google says AI features rely on the same Search systems[3], so broken foundations cap any GEO work.
  • You already show up. If engines already name you in most buyer prompts, an existing SEO and PR program may be enough. Keep monitoring, and spend on a retainer only when the numbers slip.

What does a real AI visibility audit show?

A real audit names the engines, the prompt count and the time window, and lists which pages the engines cite today. If any of those four is missing, you are looking at a sales deck.

The reason is variance. SparkToro and Gumshoe had 600 volunteers run 12 prompts through ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI a combined 2,961 times in November and December 2025[1]. They found a less than 1 in 100 chance that ChatGPT or Google's AI would return the same list of brands in any two responses, and roughly 1 in 1,000 for the same order[1]. Their conclusion was that visibility percentage "across dozens to hundreds of prompts run multiple times is a reasonable metric", while rank position is not[1].

So a credible audit looks like this:

  • Engines: each one named, for example ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, because they cite different sources.
  • Prompts: dozens at least, written the way buyers ask, split by intent (discovery, comparison, pricing, problem first), with the full list handed over.
  • Time window: repeated runs over days or weeks, with dates stated. Our own comparison used 28 prompts, run daily for 30 days[5].
  • Cited pages: the specific URLs each engine retrieves and cites for your category today, yours and other people's. This is the map the work is planned from.
  • Share of voice: how often you are named versus each competitor, as a percentage of answers.

Ask whether the audit separates mentions from citations. A brand can be named without its site being linked, and a site can be cited without the brand being recommended. Both matter, but they move for different reasons, and an agency that blends them into one score is hiding which one it can actually change.

What does a real GEO deliverable look like?

A real deliverable is mostly content and sources, not markup. Schema and llms.txt are a small part of the work, and Google says so directly: "Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add"[3]. The same guide says you don't need new "AI text files" to appear in Google Search, and don't need to break content into tiny pieces for AI[3]. Other engines publish less guidance, so treat markup as hygiene, not strategy.

What does seem to matter is the content itself. In the original GEO research paper (Princeton and others, KDD 2024), methods such as adding citations, quotations and statistics to a page lifted visibility in generative answers by up to 40%, while packing in keywords did little[4]. That was a lab benchmark from 2023 to 2024, so read it as direction, not a forecast.

In practice, a monthly deliverable from a serious agency should include:

  • A share of voice report on the agreed prompt set, per engine, with the cited sources behind each answer.
  • Owned pages rewritten or created so they answer buyer questions directly, with sourced numbers an engine can quote.
  • Work on the third-party pages engines already cite: lists, reviews, forums, trade press. In our tracking, a handful of list pages and Reddit threads drove most agency mentions[5].
  • A plain statement of what moved, what didn't, and what changes next month.

What does a GEO agency cost, and how do you measure ROI?

Our own engagements typically start around $2,000 a month, depending on the niche, with a three-month minimum; every free audit includes the specific offer, and how the plans work is on our pricing page. That is our price, not a market survey. We have not found a published, sourced study of GEO agency pricing, so we won't print a market range. Quotes far below a retainer usually mean monitoring only; quotes far above should come with a named team and named outlets.

Measure ROI the same way you would audit the agency:

  1. Baseline before signing. Share of voice per engine on a fixed prompt set, with dates.
  2. Same prompts, re-measured inside a quarter. Change the prompt set and you can no longer compare.
  3. Read movement against variance. Because single runs disagree so often[1], compare averages over a window. A jump in one screenshot is noise until repeated runs confirm it.
  4. Tie it to the pipeline where you can. AI referrals in analytics and "where did you hear about us" answers are imperfect, but they beat no link at all.

If share of voice has not moved after a quarter, the agency should say so plainly and propose a change or an exit. That conversation is a fair test of whether you hired well.

Frequently asked

What is a GEO agency?

A GEO agency works to get your brand named and cited in AI-generated answers from engines such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. Unlike SEO, which targets positions in a ranked list, GEO targets mentions inside a written answer. Good agencies measure share of voice on a fixed prompt set and work on the pages engines already cite, including third-party lists, reviews and forums.

How much does a GEO agency cost?

Redaccel engagements typically start around $2,000 a month, depending on the niche, with a three-month minimum, and each free audit includes the specific offer. We have not found a sourced market survey of GEO agency pricing, so treat any market range you see with care. Whatever the price, ask what is included: monitoring only, content work, third-party placements, or all three, and how results will be measured against a baseline.

How long does GEO take to show results?

Expect to judge results within a quarter, not a week. Engines re-crawl and re-weigh sources on their own schedules, and single answers vary so much that one run proves little. Agree a baseline before you sign, re-run the same prompts inside three months, and compare averages over repeated runs rather than screenshots. If nothing has moved by then, ask for a change of plan or stop.

Is schema markup or llms.txt enough for GEO?

No. Google's own guide says structured data isn't required for generative AI search and that no special schema.org markup or AI text files are needed to appear in Google Search. Markup is basic hygiene. The bulk of real GEO work is content that answers buyer questions with sourced facts, plus presence on the third-party pages that engines already cite for your category.
