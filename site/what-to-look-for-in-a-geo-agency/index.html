What should I look for when hiring a GEO agency?

Updated November 2026 4 min read By Redaccel
Extract redaccel.com · answer · November 2026

Look for a GEO agency that starts with an audit of which pages the AI engines actually cite for your buyers' questions, works those pages rather than publishing new ones, tracks a frozen prompt set daily, and reports share of voice per engine with a point where you can walk away. Be wary of anyone promising a position, quoting a single screenshot as proof, or selling AI visibility as a content volume play. The engines cite other people's pages far more than yours, so the agency's job is mostly to get you onto those.

1. They audit before they quote

A GEO plan written before anyone has looked at the citations is a guess. The audit should run your buyers' questions on the engines that matter to you and log every page cited. In our ChatGPT audit of one B2B category, 200 citations split into review sites 70, vendor pages 48, Reddit 42, news 12, listicles 10 and owned pages 9[1]. A proposal that does not come with that kind of table for your category is a proposal to find out on your budget. Ask to see the audit, or at least a sample from a similar category, before the price.

2. They work the cited pages, not just yours

The most-cited domains across AI engines are communities, reference sites and review platforms[2], and one "best agencies" list in our category alone was cited 562 times in 30 days[1]. An agency whose plan is mostly articles on your own site is doing the small part. Ask which third-party pages they intend to get you onto and how many times each was cited last month. If they cannot name pages, they have not done step 1.

3. They track a frozen prompt set, daily, per engine

AI answers change from run to run; in one study the same 12 prompts named different brands within the same hour across 2,961 runs[3]. The only honest measure is a share over many runs on a fixed set of questions, reported per engine, because each engine reads different pages: Google's AI Overviews take about 38 percent of their citations from the top 10 results[4], while only about 12 percent of ChatGPT's cited URLs rank there[5]. Ask three things: which prompts, which engines, and whether the set is frozen for the engagement. A set that changes month to month can show any result.

4. They say who posts on Reddit, and how

If Reddit is part of the plan, and in most categories it should be, the question is who posts and from what kind of account. Since July 2026 Reddit uses its own language models to detect and remove coordinated promotion, retroactively[6]. Comments from fresh accounts that name a brand get filtered; comments from accounts with real history mostly stay up. A good agency talks about aged accounts, subreddit rules, plain names instead of links, and what gets removed. A bad one talks about volume.

5. They put counts on the deliverables

A retainer should say how many of each thing you get a month: list placements, directory profiles, articles on cited publishers, Reddit threads answered. Which sites, which subjects and which threads can stay open, because they depend on what the engines cite that month; the counts cannot. "Ongoing optimisation" is not a deliverable.

6. They give you a point to walk away

Measurable movement in AI visibility usually takes 6 to 10 weeks from the first placements landing, so a one-month trial proves nothing and a twelve-month lock-in proves the agency does not expect to be judged. Three months with a like-for-like report at the end, and the right to leave if nothing moved, is the fair shape. We explain the timeline on how long GEO takes.

7. They refuse to promise what cannot be promised

Nobody can guarantee a position in an AI answer, because there is no fixed position to hold; there is a share of runs, and it moves. Google's own guidance for AI features is the same as for search: good content, crawlable pages, no special tricks[7]. The academic work that named the field found that adding citations, statistics and quotations to a page raised its visibility in generative answers by up to 40 percent in their tests[8], which is useful, and nowhere near a guarantee. Promises of "number one in ChatGPT" are the GEO version of "page one guaranteed".

Frequently asked

What should I look for when hiring a GEO agency?

An audit of which pages the engines cite for your buyers' questions before any quote; a plan that works those third-party pages rather than only your own site; a frozen prompt set tracked daily and reported per engine as share of voice; clarity on who posts on Reddit and from what accounts; monthly counts per deliverable; a point around month three where you can leave; and a refusal to guarantee positions.

Should a GEO agency guarantee results?

No. There is no fixed position in an AI answer to hold, only a share of runs that moves. An agency can commit to the work, the tracking and an honest report; a guaranteed mention is the GEO version of guaranteed page one.

How long should the first contract be?

Three months, with a like-for-like report at the end and the right to walk away if nothing moved. Measurable movement usually takes 6 to 10 weeks from the first placements, so one month proves nothing and twelve months removes the test.

What are the red flags?

No audit before the price, a plan that is mostly new content on your own site, screenshots as proof, no named target pages, prompt sets that change, and silence about who posts on Reddit and what gets removed.
